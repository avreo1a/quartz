
So, I learned something about myself today something I've questioned a lot while growing up. I often wondered: "Do other people experience this?", "What is it called?", and "Could this be a sign of neurodivergence?" It's a feeling, a sensation I get when my brain is deeply engaged with something I love, like skateboarding, coding, chess, building PCs, or automation. After years of curiosity, I finally decided to investigate, and I discovered it's called dopaminergic activation associated with hyperfocus. Apparently, people with ADHD often have a lower baseline dopamine level but experience a dopamine spike when intensely engaged, which can lead to stimming.

The biggest challenge I didn't anticipate is how significantly ADHD affects me as an adult. It's difficult to pinpoint its impact and find effective strategies when I'm juggling so many responsibilities: rent, credit card bills, school grades, finding a job after college, internships, LeetCode challenges, personal projects, and constantly staying current with technology, the list goes on. The resulting mental noise and cognitive load become overwhelming, significantly impacting my daily life.

To combat this, I've realized I need a dopamine detox: curbing mindless scrolling on TikTok and Reels, limiting low effort consumption habits, and reducing my reliance on AI to a healthy level. I want to shift my focus to activities that engage my mind in a more constructive way, playing games like Wordle and Sudoku, reading, writing, solving problems, and engaging in creative pursuits.
